- `/docs/scheduling/appointment-cancel` — **Appointment $cancel** — `$cancel` atomically sets a booked or pending Appointment to cancelled (with optional `cancelationReason`) and deletes its Slots, freeing the time.
- `/docs/scheduling/appointment-confirm` — **Appointment $confirm** — `$confirm` finishes find → hold → confirm by atomically moving a pending/proposed Appointment to booked and busy-tentative Slots to busy, after checking the HealthcareService is active.

- `/docs/scheduling/appointment-hold` — **Appointment $hold** — `$hold` (step 2 of find → hold → confirm/cancel) atomically creates a pending Appointment and busy-tentative Slots (multi-schedule supported) after revalidating availability, reserving time while a patient confirms or staff review.

- `/docs/scheduling/defining-availability` — **Defining Availability** — One consolidated `SchedulingParameters` extension (duration, timezone, buffers, alignment grid, slotCapacity, availability) lives on HealthcareService (shared defaults) and optionally on Schedule (per-service overrides resolved field by field). - `/docs/scheduling/state-by-state-licensure` — **Matching Patients to Providers by State-by-State Licensure** — Scheduling operations intentionally don't enforce licensure. Model one PractitionerRole per service with Locations for licensed states, resolve allowed actors via chained PractitionerRole search, and pass only their Schedules into `$find`.
- `/docs/scheduling/timezones` — **Time Zones and Timestamps** — Medplum stores FHIR dateTime values exactly as submitted, normalises internally (timestamptz) only for search and sort, and emits system timestamps in UTC. Recurring wall-clock rules need local time + timezone + recurrence, not a single UTC instant.

## Data migration and patient identity (EMPI) (`references/migration-and-identity.md`) — 18 pieces

- `/blog/empi-implementation` — **EMPI Reference Implementation** — Patient deduplication should be a human-in-the-loop pipeline: subscription-triggered candidate matching into Task + RiskAssessment, then Questionnaire-driven merges with bi-directional links, do-not-match lists, and audit.
- `/blog/patient-deduplication` — **Patient Deduplication** — Continuous deduplication rests on three things: event-driven checks at patient creation, disciplined maintenance of identifiers from every source system, and dedup policy kept as tested, source-controlled code.
- `/docs/api/fhir/operations/patient-match` — **Patient $match** — `Patient/$match` implements MPI matching grounded in the (draft) CMS Patient Matching framework's 26 approved attribute combinations, with deterministic normalisation and tightly constrained fuzziness. Disclosure mode releases a record only on a unique certain match.
- `/docs/decision-guides/data-migration` — **Data Migration Decision Guide** — A migration is defined by explicit per-domain scope, one write authority per domain, identity resolved before dependent data, versioned governed mappings, full reconciliation plus risk-based chart review, rerunnable observable loads, and rehearsed cutover. "All data" is not a scope.
- `/docs/fhir-datastore/patient-deduplication` — **Patient Deduplication Architectures** — Deduplication (the basis of an EMPI) is ingestion → matching → merging, run automatically or human-in-the-loop, always with audit reports on why records matched, why they merged, and who merged them.
- `/docs/fhir-datastore/patient-deduplication/architecture-overview` — **Architecture Overview** — Source systems feed source records into a target system, where they're matched and merged into master records. The architecture (batch vs incremental, matching rules, merge approach) depends on duplicate prevalence, source-system lifetime, cost of false positives, and the downstream application.
- `/docs/fhir-datastore/patient-deduplication/ingestion` — **Ingestion** — Start with a batch dedup pipeline (offline, N², memory-bound, often in a warehouse, exact rules to cut cardinality before fuzzy) and add incremental Bots (per-record at write time, latency-bound, fuzzier rules) as needed. Both end by updating `Patient.active` and `Patient.link`.
- `/docs/fhir-datastore/patient-deduplication/matching` — **Matching** — Use a library of matching rules ranked by false-positive rate: exact email/phone + name + gender + DOB, then name + DOB + postal code, then phonetic variants, then ML trained on human-reviewed pairs. Start with built-in `Patient/$match` (CMS framework). Record candidates as RiskAssessment and known non-matches in a Do Not Match List.
- `/docs/fhir-datastore/patient-deduplication/merging` — **Merging** — Merging is the hardest stage. Link with `Patient.active` (only the master active) and `Patient.link` (replaced-by / replaces). Choose independent master records (long-lived sources, frequent unmerge, common dupes) or record promotion (short-lived sources, rare dupes). Decide whether clinical references are rewritten to the master (app-friendly) or kept on source records (HIE provenance).
- `/docs/migration` — **Migrating to Medplum** — Migrating live operations to Medplum without interruption is mostly about explicit decisions on meaning, ownership and cutover, tested on representative data, rather than format conversion. The guide runs plan → sequence → govern mappings → convert → pipelines → validate → test → adopt/retire.
- `/docs/migration/adoption-strategy` — **Phased Adoption and Cutover** — Adopt in phases (dual write → backfill → read from Medplum behind flags → front-end writes → deprecate), with a per-phase authority matrix. Plan side effects of Subscriptions/Bots during backfill, and run a freeze → drain → backfill → verify → flip → observe → retire runbook with pre-defined rollback that preserves post-cutover writes.
- `/docs/migration/convert-to-fhir` — **Converting Data to FHIR** — Reshape source entities into FHIR resources, preserve source primary keys as identifiers, start with local codes and enrich with verified standard codes later, map values against the target binding strength, and link resources with conditional references instead of looking up Medplum IDs.
- `/docs/migration/mapping-governance` — **Governing Data Mappings** — Treat mappings as reviewed, versioned specifications, not code internals. Profile the actual source data, map meaning (not just types), govern identifier systems and terminology, give every field an explicit disposition, define one-to-many rules, record provenance, assign reviewers, and validate structure and meaning separately.
- `/docs/migration/migration-pipelines` — **Building Migration Pipelines** — Reliable pipelines use conditional updates for idempotency, batches for independent writes, transactions for atomic groups (flag required), async batches for volume. They are restartable from a manifest, authenticate as a dedicated ClientApplication (Basic auth), throttle with backoff, and treat files as a separate workstream.
- `/docs/migration/migration-planning` — **Planning Your Migration** — Before pipeline code, produce a source/domain inventory, inclusion rules, retention needs, an authority matrix (one write owner per domain per phase), identity rules, a mapping register, measurable acceptance criteria, and a cutover timeline from measured throughput. Then choose big bang, phased, parallel, or backfill-only.
- `/docs/migration/migration-sequence` — **Sequencing Your Migration** — Load shared records before their referrers: organizations/locations → practitioners/roles → patients/coverage → encounters/appointments → current clinical state → longitudinal history → documents/workflow. Adjust to your actual references.
- `/docs/migration/testing-and-acceptance` — **Testing and Accepting Migrated Data** — Acceptance needs whole-chart review (repeatable, seeded random, and risk-based samples), opened-and-verified documents, production-role testing of searches, workflows and integrations, production-scale rehearsal of performance and recovery, owned exceptions, and a named go/no-go decision.
- `/docs/migration/validation-and-reconciliation` — **Validating and Reconciling Migrated Data** — A migration is complete only when every in-scope source unit has an explained outcome (eligible = successful + rejected + skipped + quarantined), references and identity reconcile, clinical meaning is preserved (codes, units, dates, status, negation), and exceptions are owned. Keep a manifest and inspect every write result.

- `/docs/building-with-ai-coding-assistants` — **Building on Medplum with AI Coding Assistants** — The biggest quality lever for agentic engineering on Medplum is grounding the agent in Medplum's own code and docs (clone and symlink the repo). Plan first, keep threads short, verify with types, tests and server validation, and encode conventions in a rule file. Treat agent output as a draft.
